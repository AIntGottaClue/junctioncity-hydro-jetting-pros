How it works

A jetting machine pressurizes water and sends it through a flexible hose into the drain line through a cleanout or other access point. At the end of the hose is a nozzle with precisely angled orifices.

Forward-facing jets break apart the obstruction and pull the hose deeper into the line. Rear-facing jets scour the pipe wall and push loosened grease, sludge and debris back toward the access point so it can be carried out of the system entirely.

Nozzle type and pressure are matched to the job. a chain or root-cutting nozzle for a sewer lateral with root intrusion, a penetrating nozzle for a hardened grease plug, a flushing nozzle for sand and silt.

Hydro jetting vs. drain snaking

Both have a place. A cable machine is often the fastest way to restore flow. Jetting is what actually cleans the pipe.

When jetting is the right fix

  • The same drain backs up repeatedly after being snaked
  • Kitchen or commercial grease lines are running slow
  • Multiple fixtures drain slowly at the same time
  • A camera shows buildup coating the pipe wall
  • Roots have worked into a sewer lateral joint
  • Sand, silt or scale has collected in the line

When we recommend something else

Jetting is not right for every pipe. Severely corroded cast iron, collapsed sections, offset joints, and bellied lines need repair. not pressure. That is why we inspect first. If the camera shows structural damage, we will tell you plainly rather than jetting a line that cannot hold up to it.
